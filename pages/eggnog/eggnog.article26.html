Introduction
Throughout the Squirrel Initiative's many years of service, our volunteers have found that the American public is sorely undereducated when it comes to our national mammal. Undereducated people lead to underappreciated squirrels and an underfunded Initiative. But worry not, reader! The following informational pamphlet serves as an all-encompassing guide to Squirreldom! We will cover every essential topic there is to cover, and you will leave this experience as a full-blown squirrel expert.

I. Burial
The modern-day American squirrel faces many challenges, from finding nuts to storing nuts. However, these challenges pale in comparison to the overwhelming task that is crossing the road. Squirrels are a busy folk; they have little time to attend to looking both ways. It is an unfortunate reality that a common squirrel burial involves peeling its flattened corpse from the tire of a Ford F150, but it is a reality we must face. The best way to protect squirrels from an undue demise is through prevention. Drive slowly and attentively through wooded areas where squirrels may appear. The slower you drive, the more time you have to brake and the quicker your car comes to a stop. This is an essential aspect of pancake prevention! If you happen to hit the squirrel, immediately stop and exit your vehicle; there may still be time to admit CPR. Place both index fingers on the victim's chest and press firmly at a steady pace. In the event that you cannot resuscitate the squirrel, take a moment to process that you are now a murderer. This stays with you for your whole life. Every squirrel you spot will sport the stained memory of the squirrel you bashed with your car tire. Next, remove any headwear out of respect for the deceased. Then kneel beside the squirrel and repeat the following:

Forgive me, squirrel, though I am not worthy.
May your soul travel peacefully to Squirrel Heaven.
May your ancestors greet you with grace.
May your life be remembered by the forest.
Forgive me, squirrel, though I am not worthy.


A squirrel's soul cannot enter the Eternal Realm while their nuts are left hidden. To properly bury a deceased squirrel, you must unearth their stash of nuts and place them in the same hole as the corpse. Cover both with dirt, then leave a single red rose on top of the burial sight. Only then can you leave the scene of the crime and try to adjust to your new identity as a murderer.

II. Squirrel Skillets
A squirrel skillet is a flat pan that is shaped like a squirrel. We at the Initiative cannot emphasize enough that squirrel skillets are NOT meant for cooking squirrels. There are no circumstances that justify the consumption of squirrel meat. Squirrel skillets are only to be used to cook things in the shape of squirrels (i.e., squirrel-shaped pancakes). Squirrel skillets, first produced in 2026, are a highly sought-after collector's item in the squirrel-enthusiast community. Over 400,000 were produced, but we only know the location of 2,000. There are rumors of a mythical GOLDEN SQUIRREL SKILLET, said to be comprised of solid gold. However, no one has ever reported seeing such a piece.

III. References in Pop Culture
Squirrels, being a common mammal in the temperate USA, make frequent appearances in popular media. In BlueSky's Ice Age (2002), the species makes an appearance via the character of Scrat. In the film, Scrat's story serves as a comical C-plot, detailing the squirrel's misadventures attempting to capture an elusive acorn. While we appreciate Scrat's inclusion in the film, the Initiative finds that his portrayal as a clumsy and witless fool may harm the image of squirrelkind as a whole. We much more appreciate the squirrel represented in Steven Hillenberg's famous cartoon SpongeBob SquarePants (1999-present). Sandy Cheeks, a karate expert and ingenious scientist from Texas, paints a far more gracious picture of when a squirrel can strive to become. She is competitive, competent, and intelligent; everything that we believe squirrels to embody. While Cheeks is a far more positive portrayal of squirrelkind, she is a little unrealistic for our taste. She lives underwater in a tree dome, which is simply improbable. Our favorite squirrel in media is both inspirational and realistic. Pixar's Ratatouille (2007) follows the adventures of a squirrel named Remy. After being separated from his clan, he finds fulfillment in puppeteering a busboy at a fine-dining establishment in Paris. Controlling the human by tugging on his hair, he cooks up masterful dishes, reviving the reputation and spirits of his fellow cooks. Remy serves as a role model for all squirrels while remaining true to real life.

IV. Winter
No one knows where squirrels go during the winter. They have never been spotted during the months of December through February. What we do know is that squirrels store nuts specifically for the winter, meaning they are expected to survive through it. Based on our current understanding of mammals, we suspect that squirrels may go into hibernation for the winter. However, some experts are in favor of some other hypotheses. The leading theory suggests that squirrels turn white in the winter to blend in with the snow, and therefore are impossible to detect. Other studies allude to the possibility of temporary cold-weather metamorphosis. Research on this topic is ongoing and inconclusive. V. Diet
A squirrel diet consists of tree nuts.

VI. Musical Preferences
The high-strung lifestyle of squirrels can take a toll on their mental health. Constant and obsessive preparations make them prone to stress-induced seizures. To mitigate their stress, squirrels have evolved to casually enjoy classical music. From Bach to Mozart, squirrels can often be found vibing to some delicate arrangements. In a recent survey, 70% of squirrels reported listening to classical music at least one hour a week.

Besides classical music, which is universally accepted by squirrelkind, we have observed some interesting geographical patterns in relation to music taste. In densely wooded areas, we found a 50% increase in dungeon-synth listening, while more suburban areas leaned towards jazz-fusion.

Conclusion
Squirrels are truly special creatures. It is our duty to preserve the species in all ways possible. We cannot do our due diligence, however, without fully understanding squirrels. Now that you are fully educated, you wield the power to protect and serve all of squirrelkind. Thank you!
